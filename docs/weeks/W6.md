# Week 6 — modes, brainstorm behaviour, and the novelty cliff

Spec section 6, week 6:

> Mode detection + brainstorm behavior — she shuts up and takes notes on command

## Goal

Teach her that not every utterance is a request. A brainstorm is not a task list, complaining
is not a work item, and a conversation you are having with someone else in the room is not
addressed to her at all.

This is also the week spec section 10 says the magic wears off:

> **The week-6 novelty cliff.** The magic wears off and you notice how often she's subtly
> wrong. That's when the eval suite starts earning its keep. Push through.

That is a scheduling fact, not a mood. Plan for it: this week has a maintenance half as well
as a feature half, because the honest thing to do when you start noticing the errors is to
write them down and fix the retrieval, not to add another source.

## Done when

1. Saying "brainstorm" — or her detecting one — puts her in brainstorm mode, where she
   captures and does not interject.
2. Coming out of brainstorm produces a single structured note in `vault/ideas/`, not a
   running transcript.
3. `focus` suppresses every proactive surface. `away` queues instead of speaking.
4. Every mode switch, in either direction, is logged as a correction candidate.
5. You have corrected at least ten misdetections and they are in `scrutiny.db`.
6. You can talk over her and she stops within 200ms, with echo cancellation confirmed working.
7. "no, the other one" amends the task she is on; "actually, what's the weather" suspends it
   and starts a new one; "where were we" comes back to the suspended one.
8. A backchannel does not interrupt her, and speech aimed at someone else in the room resumes
   her mid-sentence rather than leaving her stopped.
9. An ambiguous interruption produces one clarifying question, not a guess.
10. The answer surface is chosen by the model: a system question is answered by voice with no
   window, "where is X" opens a map, "show me X" opens images, a data question renders a
   chart. Closeable by voice.
11. The wall display reflects actual agent state rather than an animation on a timer.
12. `make eval` still passes, and you have re-run it after the retrieval fixes this week
   produced.

## Prerequisites

- W5 complete: voice under 800ms, gate working, supervisor running, `known-good` tagged.
- Home Assistant from W4, if you want the wall display.
- A microphone with acoustic echo cancellation. ADR-0019 makes it a hard requirement for
  barge-in, and without it she interrupts herself. ESP32-S3-BOX-3 has it onboard.
- Two or three weeks of daily use behind you. Mode detection tuned against imagined
  conversations detects imaginary modes.

## Steps

### 1. Modes

`config/friday.toml` already declares them and ships with `enabled = false`:

```toml
[modes]
enabled = false
default = "assist"
available = ["assist", "brainstorm", "focus", "away"]
brainstorm_suppresses = ["act", "propagate", "ask"]
```

| Mode | She does | She does not |
|---|---|---|
| `assist` | The default. Answers, surfaces, acts within policy | |
| `brainstorm` | Captures. Asks a clarifying question only if asked one | Interject, suggest, act, surface anything |
| `focus` | Captures, and queues everything | Speak unprompted, at all |
| `away` | Captures and queues | Speak. Everything waits for your return |

Detection runs on the `router` agent: `fast`, `tools: []`, 200 tokens, temperature 0
(`config/agents.yaml`). It is a classification, and like the scorer it never picks an action
— it emits a mode and Python does the rest.

```bash
${EDITOR:-nano} config/friday.toml     # [modes] enabled = true
uv run python -m friday.modes.detect --text "right, let me think out loud for a minute"
uv run python -m friday.modes.detect --text "what time is my dentist"
```

Explicit beats inferred, always. "Brainstorm mode" as a spoken command switches immediately
and is never overridden by detection, and there is always a way to say the mode out loud. A
system where the only way to reach a mode is to be classified into it is one you cannot
drive.

### 2. Brainstorm behaviour

The done-when is "she shuts up and takes notes." Both halves.

**Shuts up.** `brainstorm_suppresses = ["act", "propagate", "ask"]` — of the five actions,
three are suppressed in brainstorm and only `watch` and `ignore` remain reachable. Note that
this is expressed in the vocabulary of the scrutiny table, which does not exist until W7;
until then the same suppression is a check in the mode handler, with the same list, so that
wiring it to scrutiny in W7 is a deletion rather than a rewrite.

**Takes notes.** Capture continuously, consolidate on exit. What comes out is one structured
note in `vault/ideas/`: the thread, the branches you abandoned and why, the decisions, and
the open questions. Not a transcript. A transcript is what a recorder produces, and if that
is all she does, a recorder is cheaper.

```bash
uv run python -m friday.modes.brainstorm --start
# ... talk for ten minutes ...
uv run python -m friday.modes.brainstorm --end
sudo -u friday cat "$(ls -1t /srv/friday/vault/ideas/*.md | head -1)"
```

Exit is explicit. A brainstorm that times out and consolidates halfway through your thought
is worse than one that runs long.

Spec section 4 lists "the end of a brainstorm session" as one of the four signal types that
gets scored. In W7 that becomes real: the note comes out of brainstorm and goes through the
scorer like any other signal, which is how a brainstorm produces follow-up without her
interrupting the brainstorm to produce it.

### 3. Barge-in

ADR-0019. You can interrupt her, and she works out whether what you said amends the task she
is on or starts a new one.

It is here rather than in W5 for a reason worth stating: this is the **same shape of problem**
as step 1. Same `router` agent, same classify-then-dispatch-deterministically pattern, same
corrections ledger. Building them in the same week is materially cheaper than building them
apart, and barge-in needs W5's graph checkpoints to already work.

**Phase 1: stop talking. Under 200ms, no model.**

```bash
uv run python -m friday.voice.bargein --check-aec
uv run python -m friday.voice.bargein --bench-stop --turns 20
```

VAD runs *during* playback and **pauses** TTS. Echo cancellation is a hard requirement, not
tuning — without it her own output re-enters the microphone and she interrupts herself, every
time, and the usual reaction is to give up on full-duplex and mute the mic during playback,
which deletes the feature. Check AEC before anything else in this step.

This deadline is not the 800ms budget. That measures end of speech to first audio out;
this measures you starting to speak to her stopping, and it needs to be roughly four times
tighter to feel like being listened to.

**Phase 2: work out what you meant. After end of speech.**

```bash
uv run python -m friday.voice.bargein --classify "no, the other Sam" --task-context last
uv run python -m friday.voice.bargein --classify "actually what's the weather" --task-context last
```

Six kinds, and the router emits a kind and a confidence — never an action:

| Kind | Example | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| `correction` | "no, the *other* Sam" | Resume from checkpoint, amended state |
| `refinement` | "and add the calendar link" | Resume, more scope |
| `abort` | "stop", "never mind" | Kill. Do not resume |
| `new_task` | "actually, what's the weather" | Suspend to checkpoint, start new |
| `unaddressed` | you, to someone else in the room | Resume as if nothing happened |
| `backchannel` | "mm-hm", "right" | Not an interruption. Keep going |

Three rules make being wrong cheap, which matters because it will be wrong:

**Pause, do not discard.** `resume_on_unaddressed` with `resume_timeout_s`. A backchannel
resumes from exactly where she stopped. Test the timeout — a pause that never resumes is
worse than no barge-in, because she stops mid-sentence and simply never finishes.

**Ambiguity asks.** Below `confidence_low`, one short clarifying question. "What about
Thursday?" is genuinely ambiguous between correcting the day and asking something new, and
that ambiguity is in the utterance rather than in the model, so no prompt removes it. One
question is far cheaper than silently doing the wrong thing to the right task.

**An unverified speaker cannot redirect.** Anyone in the room may pause her; that is physical
and fine. Only a verified voice changes what she is doing. Spec section 9 gates every voice
command and an interruption is a command.

```bash
uv run pytest tests/test_bargein.py -q
```

**Suspension is a graph checkpoint, not a new mechanism.** A `new_task` leaves the previous
run checkpointed and resumable, which is what makes "where were we" work. W5 step 5 already
built this.

One config change this forces, and it is not optional:

```bash
grep clap_exit_phrase config/friday.toml     # must NOT be "ok"
```

"ok" is the most common English backchannel. An exit phrase that collides with one asks the
classifier to separate "ok, I'm listening" from "ok, we're done" — an ambiguity in the word,
not in the model. `config/friday.toml` defaults to "stand down" and `friday.config` refuses to
load a backchannel here.

### 4. Corrections, from day one of this feature

Spec section 10:

> **Scrutiny false positives.** She'll flag your complaining as a brainstorm. Log every
> correction from day one — that's your fine-tuning set.

That sentence is about this week specifically. Complaining and brainstorming look nearly
identical to a 4B classifier: both are unstructured, both are speculative, both are addressed
at nobody.

Every mode switch is a correction candidate. `config/scrutiny.yaml` already specifies the
ledger and what a correction must contain: the decision id, the rule that fired, the seven
scores, the action taken and the action you wanted. Recording only the new mode teaches
nothing — the useful signal is which **input** produced which **misclassification**.

```bash
sqlite3 /srv/friday/db/scrutiny.db '.schema corrections'
uv run python -m friday.modes.correct --last --to assist
sqlite3 /srv/friday/db/scrutiny.db "select detected, corrected_to, count(*) from corrections group by 1,2;"
```

Ten corrections is not a lot and it is enough to see the pattern. Read them as a group rather
than fixing each one as it happens; a single misdetection is noise and five of the same shape
is a prompt change.

### 5. The output surface

Spec section 5, from cc-hermes-cc. Section 5 does not assign a week; it lands here because
this is the interaction-model week and because the wall display is the surface a mode is most
visible on.

> **AI-chosen output surface.** The model decides *where* the answer goes: system questions
> answered by voice with no window, "where is X" opens a map tile, "show me X" opens images,
> data questions render a chart. Close by voice.

`config/friday.toml` already declares it:

```toml
[output]
ai_chosen_surface = true
surfaces = ["voice", "wall", "map", "image", "chart", "text"]
default_surface = "voice"
```

Surface selection is part of the router turn, not a second model call — it is one more field
in the same classification and it costs nothing.

```bash
uv run python -m friday.output.route --text "where is the nearest hardware shop"   # map
uv run python -m friday.output.route --text "how much disk is left"                # voice
uv run python -m friday.output.route --text "show me the photos from the trip"     # image
uv run python -m friday.output.route --text "how did my sleep trend this month"    # chart
```

`default_surface = "voice"` matters: the failure mode of this feature is a window opening for
every question, and then you are operating a screen instead of talking to an assistant. Voice
with no window is the right answer more often than it feels like it should be, and every
surface must close by voice.

### 6. The wall display

Spec section 5, again from cc-hermes-cc:

> **Reactive status visualization.** Their 3D brain lights up the specific regions being
> consulted, then fires on completion. Functional progress indication disguised as spectacle.
> Whatever your dashboard looks like, make the visual reflect actual agent state.

The requirement is "actual agent state", and it is a functional requirement rather than a
decorative one. A spinner on a timer is a lie you have built into your own dashboard, and
after a week you will not trust the display to tell you whether she is working.

Emit real state and render whatever you like from it:

```bash
uv run python -m friday.output.state --follow
# {"stage":"retrieve","source":"episodic","elapsed_ms":41}
# {"stage":"rerank","candidates":60,"kept":8,"elapsed_ms":88}
# {"stage":"generate","alias":"fast","tokens":128}
```

ADR-0016 splits spec section 1's ambient row in two, and this is the half that is yours.
Home Assistant keeps the satellites, presence and IoT; the **wall surface** — Next.js,
Tailwind, shadcn/ui — owns the display of agent state.

```bash
cd wall && npm install && npm run dev     # 127.0.0.1 only; the tablet reaches it over the mesh
```

A table architecture, not a card grid. What is being shown is rows of things with a status —
running graphs and the node each is on, active loops, `ask` items waiting at a human gate,
recent decisions and the rule that produced each. Cards are for dashboards whose tiles are
unrelated; these are rows of the same kind of thing.

**It renders and never commands.** A tablet on a wall is reachable by anyone in the room, so
it is not an authenticated principal — the same threat the Resemblyzer gate exists for.
Anything consequential started from the wall goes through scrutiny's `ask` like any other
request.

Keep the mode visible at all times: in `focus` and `away` she is silent, and a silent
assistant with no visible indication of why is indistinguishable from a broken one.

### 7. The novelty cliff

The maintenance half of the week, and the reason spec section 10 names it.

You have now used this daily for over a month. The demo effect is gone and you are noticing
the subtle wrongness: an answer confident about a detail from a note that was superseded, a
person conflated with another person, a date that is off by a week.

```bash
make eval
```

If the eval still passes and she still feels wrong, the eval is not covering what is
actually wrong. That is information. Add questions — this is the one week where extending
`eval/questions.yaml` is the right move rather than the tempting one, because you are adding
questions from observed failures rather than from imagination.

```bash
${EDITOR:-nano} eval/questions.yaml     # add the failures you actually hit
make eval                                # it should now fail. Good.
```

Then fix retrieval until it passes again. The order from W3 still applies: ingestion
coverage, then chunking, then expansion, then the reranker parameters.

Push through. Spec section 10 is right that this is the week people quietly stop using the
thing they built, and it is also the week the eval suite starts earning its keep.

## Failure modes

**Complaining is classified as a brainstorm.** Predicted by name in spec section 10. Expected
this week; the fix is corrections plus a prompt change, not a different model. If it is still
happening after twenty corrections, the distinguishing feature is usually valence rather than
structure, and the prompt should say so.

**She interjects during a brainstorm "helpfully".** The suppression list is advisory
somewhere it should be enforced. `brainstorm_suppresses` must be a check in code, not a line
in a prompt. A prompt-level suppression fails exactly when the model is most confident it has
something useful.

**The brainstorm note is a transcript.** The consolidation prompt is summarising rather than
extracting. It needs a schema: thread, branches abandoned and why, decisions, open questions.

**Brainstorm times out mid-thought.** Exit must be explicit. If a timeout exists at all, make
it long and make it announce itself first.

**She interrupts herself constantly.** No echo cancellation. Her own output re-enters the
microphone, the VAD fires, she stops, and she does this every single time she speaks. The
tempting fix is to mute the mic during playback, which deletes barge-in entirely. Fix the AEC.

**She stops mid-sentence and never finishes.** `resume_on_unaddressed` off, or
`resume_timeout_s` never firing. This is worse than having no barge-in at all, because the
answer is simply lost and you have to ask again.

**"mm-hm" derails everything.** The classifier is not recognising backchannels, or
`clap_exit_phrase` is still "ok". Check the exit phrase first — it is one grep and it is the
more likely of the two.

**Every interruption is classified as a new task.** The classifier is not receiving the
in-flight task as context, so it has nothing to judge continuity against and defaults to the
safest-looking answer. Check what `--task-context` actually passes.

**A correction silently did the wrong thing to the right task.** This is the expensive failure
and the reason `ambiguity_action` is `ask`. If it happened above `confidence_low`, the
classifier was confidently wrong — log the correction and let the ratchet see it rather than
lowering the threshold.

**Someone else in the room redirected a task.** `require_speaker_match_to_redirect` is off.
Anyone may pause her; only a verified voice may change what she is doing.

**A window opens for every question.** `default_surface` is not being respected, or the
router is rewarded for choosing an interesting surface. Voice with no window is the correct
answer for most questions.

**The wall animates while nothing is happening.** The renderer is on a timer rather than on
the state stream. This is the exact failure spec section 5 warns about, and once you have
seen it you stop trusting the display entirely.

**Mode is invisible.** She is silent in `focus`, you forgot she was in `focus`, and you spend
twenty minutes debugging a working system. Put the mode on the wall.

**The eval passes and she still feels wrong.** The eval is not covering the failure. Add
questions from the failures you actually hit. This is the intended use of this week.

**You stop using her.** The most common outcome of week 6 and the one no command detects.
Spec section 10: push through.

## Do not do yet

- **No scrutiny.** W7, and it is next. The mode machinery you just built is deliberately
  written in the vocabulary of the five actions so that wiring it to the table is a deletion,
  not a rewrite.
- **No overnight autonomy.** W7. `act` and `propagate` still require you to be present.
- **No skill optimisation.** W8.
- **No new sources.** If she is subtly wrong, a fifth source makes her subtly wrong about
  more things. Fix retrieval.
- **Do not add a fifth mode.** Four cover it. A mode you enter twice a year is a mode you
  will misremember the name of.
- **Do not tune the eval questions to pass.** Add questions from real failures; never soften
  one to get back to green.
